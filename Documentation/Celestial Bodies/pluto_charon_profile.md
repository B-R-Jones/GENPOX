# Celestial Profile: Pluto & Charon (Hades & Persephone)

This document provides the scientific classification, physical characteristics, and orbital mechanics of the **Pluto-Charon Binary System**. It serves as a technical reference for binary systems, Kuiper Belt orbits, and double-synchronous tidal locking.

## 2. Physical Characteristics

Pluto and Charon form a unique system in the Solar System: their shared center of mass (barycenter) lies in open space between them. For this reason, they are technically categorized as a binary or double dwarf planet system rather than a dwarf planet and its moon.

```
       Barycenter (X)
 Pluto (O) <─── 960 km ───> [Open Space] <─────── 17,576 km ───────> (o) Charon
```

### A. Pluto Profile
*   **Mean Radius ($R_P$)**: $1,188.3 \text{ km}$ ($0.186 \text{ Earth radii}$)
*   **Mass ($M_P$)**: $1.303 \times 10^{22} \text{ kg}$ ($0.00218 \text{ Earth masses}$)
*   **Mean Density**: $1.854 \text{ g/cm}^3$ (composed of a rocky core, water ice mantle, and volatile nitrogen-methane surface ice crust)
*   **Surface Gravity ($g_P$)**: $0.62 \text{ m/s}^2$ ($0.063 \text{ g}$)
*   **Escape Velocity ($v_e$)**: $1.21 \text{ km/s}$ ($4,356 \text{ km/h}$)
*   **Atmosphere**: Extremely thin, transient atmosphere (composed of nitrogen, methane, and carbon monoxide; pressure is $10 \text{ microbar}$ at perihelion; freezes and collapses onto the surface as it moves toward aphelion).

### B. Charon Profile
*   **Mean Radius ($R_C$)**: $606.0 \text{ km}$ ($0.095 \text{ Earth radii}$)
*   **Mass ($M_C$)**: $1.586 \times 10^{21} \text{ kg}$ ($\approx 12.2\%$ of Pluto's mass)
*   **Mean Density**: $1.702 \text{ g/cm}^3$ (primarily water ice with a rock core)
*   **Surface Gravity ($g_C$)**: $0.288 \text{ m/s}^2$ ($0.029 \text{ g}$)
*   **Escape Velocity ($v_e$)**: $0.58 \text{ km/s}$ ($2,088 \text{ km/h}$)
*   **Atmosphere**: None detected (exosphere too sparse to measure).

## 3. Orbital Mechanics & Cycles

Pluto and Charon orbit each other around their shared barycenter while the system completes a highly inclined and eccentric orbit around the Sun.

### A. Mutual Pluto-Charon Orbit
*   **Barycentric Distance**: $19,596 \text{ km}$ (average distance between centers)
*   **Mutual Orbital Period ($T_m$)**: $6.3872 \text{ days} \quad (6\text{d } 9\text{h } 17\text{m } 34\text{s})$
*   **Eccentricity**: $0.00005$ (nearly perfectly circular)
*   **Double Synchronous Lock**: Both Pluto and Charon are tidally locked to each other. Pluto always presents the same hemisphere to Charon, and Charon always presents the same hemisphere to Pluto. This is the only major system in the Solar System to exhibit mutual tidal locking.

### B. Sun-System Orbit Parameters
*   **Semi-major Axis ($a$)**: $5,906,380,000 \text{ km}$ ($39.482 \text{ AU}$)
*   **Perihelion (Closest Approach)**: $4,436,820,000 \text{ km}$ ($29.658 \text{ AU}$)
*   **Aphelion (Furthest Point)**: $7,375,930,000 \text{ km}$ ($49.305 \text{ AU}$)
*   **Orbital Eccentricity ($e$)**: $0.2488$
*   **Orbital Inclination ($i$)**: $17.16^\circ$ relative to the ecliptic plane.
*   **Sidereal Period ($T_s$)**: $90,560 \text{ days} \quad (248.00 \text{ years})$
*   **Synodic Period ($T_y$)**: $366.73 \text{ days}$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Orbital Resonance**: Pluto is in a $2:3$ mean-motion resonance with Neptune. For every three orbits Neptune completes, Pluto completes two. This resonance prevents collisions despite Pluto's orbit crossing inside Neptune's orbital path.

## 4. System Obliquity & Chaos

*   **Retrograde System Tilt**: The Pluto-Charon rotational axis and their mutual orbital plane are tilted at $122.53^\circ$ to their orbital plane around the Sun. The system rotates in a retrograde direction and virtually lies on its side, similar to Uranus.
*   **Minor Satellites**: The Pluto-Charon system has four small, irregular outer moons: **Nix**, **Hydra**, **Kerberos**, and **Styx**. Due to the fluctuating gravitational fields of the central binary, these outer moons do not exhibit tidal locking. Instead, they tumble chaotically as they orbit the barycenter.
*   **Tholin Reddening**: Solar UV radiation reacts with nitrogen and methane on Pluto's surface to create complex organic polymers called **tholins**, which accumulate and stain the equatorial regions of Pluto and Charon's northern polar cap (the *Mordor Macula*) a deep reddish-brown.
